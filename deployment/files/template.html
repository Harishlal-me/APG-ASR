    What it doesn't do

    The report states its limits without qualification, so this page does too.

    1. Aggregate accuracy doesn't rise.mAP@[0.5:0.95] is 0.2135 with and without refinement. APG-ASR is a complementary layer, not a stronger detector.
    2. Not every refinement helps.33.8% of modified boxes moved away from the ground truth. Non-degradation is an empirical observation, not a guarantee.
    3. It is slower.121.9 ms per frame against roughly 45 ms, with a worst case of 176.6 ms. It is not suited to real-time use.
    4. Narrow evidence.One dataset (COCO 2017), one backbone, and a 40,000-image training subset on a single GPU. Generality is plausible but not demonstrated.
    5. The ceiling is an argument, not a proof.The bias-limited hypothesis is a modelling argument. It predicts what the measurements show, and it has not been formally proven.

    Why consensus can't move the ceiling

    Each scale's box is the truth plus a shared bias plus its own noise. Agreement across scales averages the noise away. The bias is identical in every pass, so it stays.

    Check what you took away

    Six quick questions on the method, then the questions a reviewer is most likely to ask.

    Why not just average the three scales?
    Averaging correlated estimates does not cancel their shared error, and it produces a box that may match none of the passes. It tends to be larger and blurrier. Consensus selection adopts a box that some pass of the detector actually produced.
    Why is the confidence score frozen?
    Variants V3 and V4 accept the identical 1,716 boxes. V3 writes the new score and mAP@50 drops by 0.0019. V4 keeps the old score and the metric is unchanged. The score change alone re-ranks detections, so freezing it makes geometry the only thing that can move a metric.
    Is the "agent" a learned policy?
    No. It is a deterministic selection mechanism with no learned policy and no randomness. Given the same inputs and parameters it always makes the same decision.
    Does it improve mAP?
    Not in aggregate. mAP@[0.5:0.95] is 0.2135 with and without refinement. What improves is the geometry of the boxes it touches: 55.8% move closer to the ground truth against 33.8% that move away.
    Why does a strong detector benefit less?
    A strong detector leaves less random localisation error for consensus to remove. With the pretrained detector the effect is small, and refinement acts mainly as a stability layer. With the weaker custom-trained detector, gains reach +0.28 IoU under occlusion.
    Can I use it on another detector or dataset?
    The acceptance rule is not specific to COCO or this backbone, so it should apply more broadly. That has not been demonstrated, and the report makes no claim of validated generality.

      APG-ASR: How to explore

      Welcome to the interactive showcase for APG-ASR! Here is how to navigate the research:

      1. 1. Compare: Drag the slider on the images to instantly compare a standard YOLOv8 detector against our APG-ASR refined version. Notice how confident boxes are protected while uncertain ones are improved.
      2. 2. Pipeline: Scroll down to follow a single image through the entire architectural flow. Click "Play Auto-Flow" to watch the agent step through refinement.
      3. 3. Policy Lab: Experiment with our acceptance thresholds yourself. Change the rules and watch how the precision/recall metrics react in real-time.
      4. 4. Results: Review the aggregate dataset metrics from the paper.

      Tip: Use Ctrl K to open the command palette for quick navigation.
